Executive Director

Matthew S.W. Silk

Mount Allison University

Dr. Matt Silk is an Assistant Professor of Philosophy and the founding director of the Institute. He completed his PhD in philosophy at Waterloo and co-authored An Inquirer's Guide to Ethics in AI (Broadview Press). His work focuses on responsibility and inductive risk in emerging technologies, and he hosts the Ethics of AI Podcast.

Secretary / Treasurer

Samantha St. Amand

University of Waterloo

A Data Scientist at the University of Waterloo with over a decade of experience in analytics and statistical modelling. Her background in economics, global governance research, and institutional data integration informs a commitment to multidisciplinary, evidence-based evaluation of emerging technologies.

Director-at-large

Kem-Laurin Lubin

University of Waterloo

Dr. Kem-Laurin Lubin is a rhetorician of technology whose research examines how artificial intelligence systems characterize the people they act upon, a process her dissertation named algorithmic ethopoeia, and how people can be taught to read and answer that characterization. She holds a PhD from the University of Waterloo (2025) and spent nearly two decades in the technology industry as a human-centred design researcher, design leader, and certified design thinking coach before returning to the academy.

She is the author of User Experience in the Age of Sustainability: A Practitioner's Blueprint (Elsevier, 2012) and Design Heuristics for Emerging Technologies: AI, Data, and Human-Centered Futures (Sage/Universal Write Publications, 2025); her third book, Rhetoric in the Africana Tradition, is forthcoming from Bloomsbury (2027).

She serves as Executive Director of the AI Global South Summit, a roving summit centring the conversations of those living under Global South conditions irrespective of place; she is a co-founder of the Human Futures AI Training Institute; and she is an incoming postdoctoral fellow of the Digital Policy Hub at the Centre for International Governance Innovation.

Her scholarship appears with Taylor & Francis, in IEEE Technology and Society Magazine, and in a co-authored Springer volume; her public writing on AI, rhetoric, and society reaches thousands of readers weekly; and she contributes regularly to municipal and national conversations on AI and data governance. As Director-at-Large, she holds the Institute's seat where the humanities meet the machine.
